02 Your four levers, in concrete terms

For each lever, describe what your business specifically offers a strong senior candidate. Concrete and defensible — not generic.

Scope

At a big company, a senior person owns a slice. At yours, they own a whole function — and they own it visibly.

Proximity to the owner

Working directly with the principal. Weekly time, real input into direction.

Speed

Decisions in days, not quarters. Ideas get tried. The work has visible consequences.

A real stake in what they build

Not necessarily equity. A meaningful share in what they help build — structured well enough that they feel it.

03 The three-part pitch

Sell the seat, not the company. Senior candidates' tolerance for vague pitches is exactly inverse to their seniority.

PART 1
What the role owns

Scope, team, decisions, budget. Specifics. Not "wear many hats" — that tells a senior candidate you haven't done the work.

PART 2
What the first 90 days look like

Named priorities. The people they will work with. The decisions you expect from them.

PART 3
Where the role goes in two years

The real path. What success would mean for compensation, scope, responsibility. Honest. Defensible.
