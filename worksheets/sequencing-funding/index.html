STEP ONE · YOUR BUSINESS

A few details about where you are.

Honest numbers, even rough ones, beat polished guesses. The next page will diagnose your stage from your answers — not from your self-assessment.

THE PRINCIPLE THAT GOVERNS THE WHOLE WORKSHEET
Most owners don't sequence funding. They react.
When owners react, the most expensive money — equity, high-interest credit, emergency debt — tends to be what's available when you're under pressure. The cheapest, most owner-friendly capital — revenue, founding clients, a line of credit established in advance — tends to be what you bypass when things feel urgent. This worksheet is calibrated to keep you sequencing instead of reacting.

STEP TWO · STAGE DIAGNOSTIC

Six gate questions. Answer honestly.

Each pair of questions tests whether you've cleared the gate to a stage. The diagnostic places you at the most advanced stage you've actually unlocked — not the one you'd like to be in.

GATES TO STAGE 1 · REVENUE TRACTION
Has the model been proven by paying customers?
Do you have paying customers right now — not pilots, not promises?

Money in the bank from people who chose to pay you for the offer.

Can you describe — clearly and consistently — what those customers are paying you for?

If you'd describe it differently to two different prospects, the offer isn't yet defined enough.

GATES TO STAGE 2 · FOUNDING CLIENTS & SELF-FUNDING
Are you extending runway through customer relationships and design?
Have you structured at least one founding client agreement (prepaid or committed revenue tied to insider terms)?

Not "interested clients." Signed agreements with founding-client framing.

Have you tightened payment terms or improved margin mix in a deliberate way?

Net 15 instead of net 30. A higher-margin tier introduced. Drop-ship instead of inventory. Concrete moves you've actually made.

GATES TO STAGE 3 · CREDIT AS A BRIDGE
Is revenue predictable enough to borrow against?
Has revenue been predictable enough that you can forecast next month within 15%?

Not "I hope we'll be at $X." Actual evidence from the last 3-6 months that lets you predict.

Do you have a line of credit set up before you need it — and the discipline to leave it untouched until a draw passes the two-question test?

A line that exists, with rules, with you not using it for operating costs. If you don't have one yet, that's a no.

GATES TO STAGE 4 · OUTSIDE CAPITAL
Is the model demanding it — not your stress, not an investor's email?
Is the model proven — real revenue, real customers, clear understanding of what drives growth?

You're not funding a hypothesis. You're funding a machine you already know how to operate.

Do you have a specific growth opportunity with a defined window that your current funding cannot capture?

A specific use of capital with a return timeline. "Some growth I'll get to eventually" doesn't qualify.

Please answer all eight gate questions before continuing.

STEP THREE · YOUR RAISE SIGNAL

The condition that tells you outside capital is the right next move.

Three components. Define them now, calmly, while things are stable. The raise signal is your protection against the moment when desperation makes any term sheet look reasonable.

WHAT QUALIFIES AS A RAISE SIGNAL · WHAT DOESN'T
QUALIFIES: A market window that closes. A competitor accelerating their moves. A specific revenue milestone that unlocks a discrete growth investment with a measurable return timeline.

DOES NOT: "Some growth I'll get to eventually." A feeling of being tight on cash. An investor opportunity that appeared in your inbox. Stress. Urgency. FOMO.
COMPONENT 01 · THE SPECIFIC TRIGGER
The observable condition that flips the switch.

A specific revenue milestone, a team-size threshold, a market opportunity with a defined window. Not a feeling. A measurable event you'll know when you see.

"When monthly recurring revenue passes $50K and a regional competitor announces a national expansion that puts our 12-month window at risk."

COMPONENT 02 · THE COST OF NOT RAISING
If the trigger fires and you don't raise, what specifically do you forgo?

This is the test from the lesson: "the cost of not raising was higher than the cost of raising." You need to be able to name what you'd lose. If you can't, the trigger isn't really a trigger.

"The 18-month head start in the western region. Once the competitor moves, the cost to acquire customers there triples and we lose the owner-friendly press cycle."

COMPONENT 03 · WHAT THE CAPITAL FUNDS
Specific destinations. Not a general operating budget.

Each item should have a projected return timeline. The script is direct: "capital had specific destinations, not a general operating budget."

"A complete sales team (5 hires, 9-month payback). Expanded marketing in the western region (12-month payback). One product launch tied to a confirmed channel partner (6-month payback)."
