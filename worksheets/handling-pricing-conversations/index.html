STEP ONE · YOUR PRICE AND VALUE ANCHOR

The one sentence that frames what they're evaluating.

The value anchor matters because without it, the buyer compares your price to whatever else they've seen lately. With it, they're evaluating the price against the actual problem you solve.

THE PATTERN THIS WORKSHEET PROTECTS AGAINST
Negotiating against yourself before anyone has objected.
It's the most common pricing mistake owners make after the price is built. The number is right. The delivery is wrong. owners discount, apologize, or over-explain before the buyer raises a single concern — and they don't realize they're doing it because to them, it feels like being helpful or transparent. Every qualifier says the same thing: the owner isn't sure the price is right. And if the owner isn't sure, why would the buyer be?
THINGS THAT SOUND HELPFUL · THAT ACTUALLY UNDERMINE YOUR PRICE
"The kit is $89 — but we're happy to be flexible." → Nobody asked. You just told them the price is negotiable.
"It's $89 for now, though that may change as we figure things out." → You just told them the price is provisional.
"I know that might seem like a lot for skincare." → They hadn't thought that. Now they have.

STEP TWO · THE EXACT DELIVERY SENTENCE

Write it once. Then say it the same way every time.

Variation is a tell. When you say the price slightly differently in each conversation — hesitating here, qualifying there — buyers register it. Not consciously, but they sense you don't have a settled relationship with your own price. Settle it now.

SELF-CHECK · READ YOUR SENTENCE OUT LOUD
Is any of this in there?

Read your sentence aloud — at speaking pace, the way you'd actually say it to a buyer. If any of these are present, the sentence isn't done yet.

⚠ "But" — as in "$89 but we can be flexible." Cut it.
⚠ "Just" — as in "it's just $89." Apologetic. Cut it.
⚠ "For now" — as in "$89 for now." Tells them the price is provisional. Cut it.
⚠ "I know" — as in "I know that might seem like a lot." Plants the objection they hadn't formed. Cut it.
⚠ "Sorry" or "unfortunately" — there is nothing to apologize for. Cut them.
STEP THREE · THE THREE PUSHBACKS

Three responses. Decided in advance.

When pushback comes — and it will — the worst response is an immediate discount or a defensive explanation. Both signal the same thing: the price wasn't really firm. These are the three pushbacks every owner hears, and the responses that hold the price without breaking the relationship.

PUSHBACK 01 · "THAT'S MORE THAN WE EXPECTED"
Contextualize. Don't defend.

The lesson is direct: don't defend with cost-justification. Use market data instead. Where does your price sit in the comparable range?

A real range you can defend. "Comparable [category] runs $X to $Y." If you don't know the range, that's the first thing to find out before this conversation.

"Comparable clinical-grade skincare in this category runs $95 to $130. We're at the lower end of that range, intentionally — for founding practices."

PUSHBACK 02 · "WE'D NEED A LOWER PRICE"
Don't discount. Diagnose.

This is where most owners immediately discount. Don't. Ask the question first — it's often not the per-unit price that's the real problem. It's order quantity, payment timing, or uncertainty about demand. You can't solve a problem you haven't identified.

A question that opens the real concern. Standard form: "What's making this difficult at $X?" Adapt to your voice.

"What's making this difficult at this price — is it the per-unit cost, the order quantity, or the timing of the payment?"

PUSHBACK 03 · "LET US THINK ABOUT IT"
This is not a no. It's "we need more information or more time."

The wrong response is to push for a yes now. The right response keeps the conversation moving without applying pressure.

An offer of help, not an ask for closure. What would actually help them decide?

"Absolutely. What would be most helpful for me to send over while you're considering it — case studies, the clinical outcomes data, references from current practices?"
