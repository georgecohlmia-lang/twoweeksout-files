Build Your Scalable Systems Roadmap

Build the systems that enable your next stage — not the ones that optimize a stage you haven't reached.
1

Your business — at a glance

Where you are now. Systems decisions only make sense against your current stage.
An honest snapshot of where the duct tape is. This is what your systems roadmap will address.
2

The enable-vs-optimize test

A system is worth building if it enables your next stage. It's premature if it only optimizes this one.
Build it — it enables
Enabling systems
Without it, you cannot reach the next stage. It unlocks growth you can't otherwise reach. Build these slightly ahead of need.
Hold it — it only optimizes
Optimizing systems
It makes the current stage smoother, faster, prettier — but you'd grow fine without it. Premature scaling lives here. Hold until proof.
You can't decide what enables the next stage until you've named the next stage. Be specific — a stage, not a wish.
A concrete, near-term stage: a revenue level, a team size, a market, a customer count.
The things that work now but won't survive the jump. These point straight at your enabling systems.
3

The systems inventory

List every system you're considering. Classify each: build now, or hold for proof.
Include AI and automation here too — judged by the same test. AI that's a productivity multiplier today may be "build now"; AI as an optimization layer for scale you don't have yet is "hold for proof." Same lens, no exception.

4

Name the proof trigger

For every "hold for proof" system: the specific signal that flips it to build.
A held system without a trigger is just a system you forgot. The trigger is a specific, observable signal — a number, a hire, a milestone — that says "the proof has arrived; now build it."
5

The 90-day systems plan

What you build first, who owns it, and what you're explicitly NOT building yet.
Of the systems you classified "build now," the single one to start in the next 90 days.
A specific, observable outcome — not "CRM implemented" but what it lets you do.
Name the held systems. Naming what you're refusing is how you avoid premature scaling.
