Whether you're stewarding an inherited culture or shaping one from zero, the discipline is the same: read what's there or forming, decide what to keep or change, and commit to what you'll no longer tolerate.

01 Your starting position

The work is the same. The vocabulary differs. Name where you are so the artifact reads honestly.

02 Read what is there — or what is forming

Before you change a culture, you read it. Three lenses — observable in the first 60–90 days if you're looking.

Artifacts

How meetings actually run. How people communicate when the owner is not in the room. How clients are spoken about. How mistakes are handled.

Patterns

Who gets promoted. Who gets praised in front of the group. Who quietly leaves and why. The unwritten rules of who succeeds here.

Residue

For the acquirer: the prior owner's habits and language still shaping behavior. For the greenfield owner: your own residue from your last company — the defaults you're unconsciously importing.

04 Operating norms — 3 to 5 that actually run the place

Not aspirational posters. Clear principles the team can use to make decisions when you're not in the room. The test: could two people read it and reach the same conclusion about a specific situation?

The norm (one sentence)
The two-people test — what specific situation does this clarify?
05 Psychological safety — honest tests

Not "no one feels bad." A team where people can disagree, raise problems, admit mistakes, and ask questions without being punished.

The problem test

When something goes wrong, does the team surface it early — or stay hidden until it explodes?

The disagreement test

When you say something the team disagrees with, does anyone say so? If no one does, ever, you have politeness — not safety.

What you're committing to as the owner

One specific behavior change. The most common: ask "what am I missing?" — and mean it, including when the answer is uncomfortable.

06 Multi-location considerations (optional)

Only if you operate — or are planning to operate — across more than one location.

Owner-presence cadence per location

Predictable physical presence — not constant, but enough that being in a non-headquarters office doesn't feel like being forgotten.

The rituals that travel

Posters don't cross city lines. Rituals do. Name the practices you'll run consistently across locations.

07 What you will stop tolerating

Culture is what you tolerate, repeated. One specific pattern you've been letting pass that you're going to start intervening on — beginning this week.
